AI-Assisted Software Project · Individual Capstone

AI Canva — "One Student, One Box"

Design, research, and build your own AI workflow box — and justify why it exists.
Mode: Individual
Build: 30%
Research: 70%
Demo: part of Build

1. Overview

AI Canva is a visual whiteboard where users place boxes (Idea, Research, PRD, Code, Slides…) on a canvas, connect them, and let AI content flow from box to box to turn an idea into a working prototype.

You will design and build one new box/workflow of your own, using AI assistance. The build is achievable by one person — the real heart is the research: what problem your box solves, why your design choices are right, what alternatives you considered, and the ethical implications.

At the end, every student's box connects into a shared class "mega-pipeline" — one giant flow from idea to prototype assembled from everyone's contributions.

2. What you build (and how it plugs in)

Each box connects to the AI Canva scaffold through three touchpoints you control:

#TouchpointWhat it isWhat you do
1types.ts entryThe box's "identity card"Add a BoxType + BOX_TYPES entry: label, icon, color, category, prompt
2runBox branchOrchestrator in boardStore.tsGather connected inputs, call the AI backend, post-process
3Prompt templateYour prompt-engineeringWrite a defaultPrompt using {{inputs}}, {{input_1}}, {{Box Name}}

Optional: a custom render in BoxNode.tsx (score bars, cards, a banner) and/or a backend endpoint if your box needs a non-text provider.

Demo: a working box + a small sample pipeline (e.g. Idea → [your box] → Summarise) proving it reads inputs, produces useful output, and composes with other boxes.

4. Deliverables

A. Working box (Build)

  • types.ts entry for your box
  • runBox branch that reads inputs and produces output
  • A prompt template using {{inputs}} / {{input_1}} / {{Box Name}}
  • A working demo pipeline (your box + upstream + downstream)
  • Clean, readable, commented code

B. Written research report (Research) — 2,000–2,500 words:

  1. Problem & motivation
  2. Domain context (cite sources)
  3. Design rationale (prompt, output, provider)
  4. Alternatives considered
  5. Ethical & social considerations
  6. Evidence-based answer to the research question

7. Guidelines

  • Use AI to build — but be able to explain every part of your box.
  • Don't over-engineer — a working, well-researched simple box beats a broken complex one.
  • Cite everything — reference the research you justify with.
  • Be honest about limits — acknowledge what the AI can't do, not just what it can.
Good luck. Your box becomes part of the shared class mega-pipeline. The quality of your research is what makes it a genuine contribution. Go explore. Go build. Go justify.
